Most short memory tests were built to detect dementia. By the time they catch it, you've often lost three to five years.
The MCI Screen is different. It was built specifically to catch mild cognitive impairment — the much earlier stage, when there's still time to act on reversible causes, lifestyle, and new treatments.
Why most screens miss it

Built for the question you're actually asking.

The two screens most doctors use — the MMSE and the MoCA — flag mild cognitive impairment correctly in fewer than two-thirds of cases when used in primary care.

That means a lot of people who walk in worried walk out reassured, when they shouldn't have been. And a lot of healthy people walk out scared.

The MCI Screen was engineered to fix that gap. It uses tasks weighted specifically for the earliest stage of cognitive change — the stage where intervention works.

Published evidence

The science, in black and white.

Four independent peer-reviewed studies. Published across the most cited journals in cognitive neuroscience. Open the original papers if you want — the math is all on the table.

PNAS 2005

Methods to improve the detection of mild cognitive impairment

Shankle WR, Romney AK, Hara J, Fortier D, Dick MB et al.
Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, vol. 102, no. 13, 4919–4924.
Established the underlying algorithm. Demonstrated >95% sensitivity and specificity for distinguishing MCI from normal aging in a primary-care population.
Journal of Alzheimer's Dis. 2007

Detecting cognitive impairment in primary care: performance assessment of three screening instruments

Trenkle DL, Shankle WR, Azen SP.
Journal of Alzheimer’s Disease, vol. 11, 323–335.
Head-to-head comparison vs MMSE, MoCA, Mini-Cog and Clock Drawing. The MCI Screen produced the highest combined sensitivity and specificity — 97% accuracy in the cohort.
J. Psychiatr. Res. 2009

A meta-analysis of the accuracy of the MMSE in the detection of dementia and MCI

Mitchell AJ.
Journal of Psychiatric Research, vol. 43, 411–431.
Pooled analysis of dozens of studies. Confirmed the MMSE’s well-known weakness for MCI — 71% sensitivity, ~36% specificity for separating MCI from normal aging.
Cochrane Review 2015

MoCA for the detection of dementia within a community setting

Davis DHJ, Creavin ST, Yip JLY et al.
Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, Issue 10. CD010775.
Independent Cochrane review. In primary-care populations, MoCA specificity falls to 60% or below — meaning roughly 4 in 10 healthy people are wrongly flagged.
What the screen actually measures

Four cognitive domains. Not a single guess.

Each domain is tested through tasks adapted from the CERAD Word List, the gold-standard battery used in clinical Alzheimer’s research worldwide. Your score on each is age- and education-adjusted before the final result.

01 — Memory

Short-term recall

Hearing a list of words and recalling them after a brief delay — the single most sensitive test for early MCI in the published literature.

Task: 10-word list, delayed recall
02 — Executive function

Planning & sequencing

Quickly organizing actions in the right order. This is where some of the earliest, subtlest changes show up — before memory itself feels different.

Task: Executive clock task
03 — Judgment

Pattern & problem solving

Making sense of a situation and choosing what to do. Slips here often appear in everyday life as “not like myself” long before a diagnosis.

Task: Scenario judgment items
04 — Language

Word retrieval

Pulling specific words out on demand. Trouble finding the right word is one of the most common early experiences people describe.

Task: Naming & semantic fluency

Common questions

Things people ask before they start.

Will this tell me if I have Alzheimer's?
No. It's a screen, not a diagnosis. It tells you whether your memory looks within the normal range for your age. If your result suggests a closer look, your report will tell you exactly what to do next.
Is it really private?
Yes. No account needed. Your responses are stored under HIPAA-aligned privacy. Nothing is ever sold. You decide whether to share your result with your doctor.
How long does it take?
About three minutes for the Quick Memory Check. Twenty to thirty minutes for the full Risk Profile. Both can be done in one sitting or paused and finished later.
What if I'm taking this for my mother or father?
That's a common reason people start. There's a short version where you answer on their behalf, which is often the most accurate way for a worried family member to find out where things stand.
How is this different from a test at the doctor's office?
Most quick cognitive screens used in doctor's offices were designed to detect dementia. By the time they're positive, you may have lost years. This was built to catch mild cognitive impairment — the much earlier stage where there's still time to act.
What does my doctor see if I share it?
A one-page clinical summary: the Memory Performance Index, the sub-scores by cognitive domain, the modifiable risk factor breakdown, and a recommended next step. Designed to be read in under a minute. PDF or FHIR-format export available.
